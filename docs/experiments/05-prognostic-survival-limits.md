# Experiment 05: Prognostic of Survival and Biological Limits

## Context and Objective
After establishing reactive vision and GABAergic inhibition for the camera interface, the system presents autonomous stability. This document serves to register my hypotheses and theoretical projections about how far this connectome can survive in the game nights before biological fidelity becomes an insurmountable bottleneck.

## Reflections per Difficulty Stage

### Nights 1 and 2: The Reactive Threshold
I believe the purely reactive model built so far is more than sufficient to pass these nights. Bonnie and Chica rely exclusively on simple visual door checks. If the Mean Squared Error (MSE) remains stable against false positives, the fly should survive purely on startle instinct.

### Night 3:
This night in specific is interesting and complexity shifts here. Foxy does not attack via door lights, because he requires the player to open the monitor periodically to delay his run. Since the current model is purely reactive to door events, the fly would invariably be killed.
My hypothesis for the future is that it might be necessary to implement a biological rhythm circuit, such as a Central Pattern Generator. If I can simulate a CPG that forces the fly to check the camera instinctively at semi-regular intervals, it might be possible to hold Foxy back without breaking neurobiological immersion.

### Nights 4 and 5:
This is where I suspect nature will fail against the game design. Freddy requires active tracking and energy consumption becomes punitive.
A real fly lacks the necessary cognition to manage resources long-term (such as saving power to avoid dying in the dark at 5 AM, because it doesn't have long-term memory naturally). To beat these nights in a purely biological way, I will probably have to design a metabolic fatigue pathway. Something like simulating ATP exhaustion that physically inhibits the fly from overusing the light, forcing energy conservation. But honestly, it seems really difficult, and I'd only do anything like that if i have free time, because i think a fly surviving for 4 whole nights is already magnificent.

### Night 6 and 4/20 Mode:
Realistically, I doubt it is possible to beat 20/20/20/20 mode without turning the simulation into a conventional bot. Human players beat these nights by executing robotic and blind motor loops timed to the fraction of a second.
The fly connectome possesses stochastic noise, synaptic delays, and an inherently chaotic reactivity. This biological chaos would very likely cause a loss due to power drain or a millisecond timing failure against Freddy. I believe pure untrained biology reaches its mathematical limit before 4/20 mode.

## Next Steps
Keep track of practical tests to confirm if the Night 3 barrier really demands the CPG, or if the stochastic noise of the current foraging loop manages by chance to delay Foxy enough.
